Friends & family guide

Read the brief, check the setup, then decide for yourself.

Futures Desk is one trader's daily research, shared with a small circle. This page explains what you're looking at and how to use it well.

01What this is

Every trading day, a research process reads the economic calendar, overnight market moves and the news, then writes a short brief with a handful of trade setups in futures, stocks, ETFs and options. It blends the economic backdrop, scheduled catalysts and chart structure, and every idea goes through the same checks before it's published.

Ideas, not instructions. Every setup is a hypothesis to check against live prices and your own judgment. Nobody here is a licensed adviser, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.

04Scores and grades

Every setup is scored out of 10 on the same checklist: whether it suits the economic backdrop, whether there's a catalyst coming, what the trend and chart location look like, how crowded the trade already is, whether the reward is worth the risk, and whether related markets agree. Only setups that clear a minimum score are published as trades; the rest stay on the watch list.

  • A 8–10: the strongest setups. Rare.
  • B 6–7: worth considering, usually at half size or after confirmation.
  • C 5 or less: a watch, not a trade.

05Regime and mode

Each brief names a regime: roughly where the economy sits in the business cycle. Different markets tend to do well in each one.

RegimeUsually helpsUsually hurts
Early recoveryStocks, small caps, copperGold, the dollar
Mid-cycleTech stocks, oilBonds
Late cycle / overheatingOil, gold, grainsTech, small caps, bonds
SlowdownBonds, gold, the yenSmall caps, copper, oil

The regime comes from a gauge built on public economic data, updated every weekday. The Crowded trades panel flags markets where speculators are unusually one-sided; crowded trades can unwind fast on a surprise, so setups on the crowded side are marked down.

The mode tells you how markets are treating economic news. In "good news is good" mode, strong data lifts stocks. In "good news is bad" mode, strong data hurts stocks, because traders expect the Fed to keep rates higher for longer.

06Release reactions

Some reports move markets hard: jobs, inflation, retail sales, business surveys, oil inventories, crop reports and Fed decisions. When one lands, a release update appears at the top of the Futures tab.

  • Actual vs. expected: what came out compared with what economists expected. The size of the surprise is what moves prices.
  • Reaction: how each market moved in the first minutes.
  • Morning ideas: whether each earlier setup is confirmed or cancelled.
The first move often reverses. The desk waits for the direction that holds before acting on a release.

06bThe economic cheat sheet

The Cheat sheet tab covers the major US economic reports. For each one it shows what the report is, when it comes out, what part of it markets watch, and how different markets have tended to react when it comes in hotter or cooler than expected.

History, not a guarantee. These are averages of past release days. Use them to know what to watch, not as a reason on their own to place a trade.

07Sector rotation

The stock market is split into 11 sectors, each tracked by a fund (XLK for tech, XLE for energy, and so on). The sector scan sorts them into four groups by recent performance:

  • Leading strong over the past month and the past week
  • Improving weak for the month, turning up this week
  • Weakening strong for the month, fading this week
  • Lagging weak over both

When the leaders match what the regime predicts, the brief's view gets more weight. When they don't, it's a warning sign.

08The trade journal

This tab shows trades the desk actually took, win or lose, with the setup score each had. Results are shown in R: +2R means the trade made twice what it risked, and −1R means it hit its stop.

Separately, every setup is graded automatically after each close, whether anyone traded it or not. Each ticket gets a Result line (waiting for entry, in trade, target hit, stopped out, time exit or never triggered), and the Scorecard tab totals them by grade, market and timeframe. Grading uses real daily futures prices and counts a day that touched both stop and target as a loss, so results lean conservative.

That grading doubles as a paper-trading ledger: every setup is treated as one contract of the market it names (100 shares for ETFs and stocks), with no fees. The top of the Scorecard shows closed and open paper profit, the best and worst trades, and an equity curve. Compare setups by R (multiples of the risk taken) rather than dollars, because a full-size gold contract swings far more dollars than a micro S&P.

09Rating and comments

  1. Tap Rate & comment on any setup.
  2. Pick 1–5 stars for how much you like the idea.
  3. Add your name and a short comment, such as "Would wait for the CPI number first" or "Stop looks tight to me".
  4. Tap Post. Everyone with the code sees it within seconds.

Comments are encrypted with the access code, so only people in the circle can read them. Be kind and specific; the point is better decisions.

11Risk basics

Futures are leveraged: a small move in price is a large move in your account, and losses can exceed what you deposited. If you're new, these rules are the ones that matter most.

  • Paper trade first. Follow the setups on a simulator for a month before risking real money.
  • Use micros. A micro contract is one-tenth the size of the full one. One MES moves $5 per S&P point instead of $50.
  • Risk a small, fixed slice. Many traders risk 0.5–1% of the account per trade.
  • Set the stop before you enter, and never move it further away.
  • Size from the stop. Example: a $10,000 account risking 1% has $100 to risk. An MES trade with a 10-point stop risks $50 per contract, so that's 2 contracts.

12Questions

I forgot the access code, or it stopped working.

Ask the person who invited you. The code is changed now and then, and the site will say so when yours is out of date.

Can I add it to my phone's home screen?

Yes. On iPhone, open it in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen. With "Remember on this device" ticked, it opens straight to the desk.

Why is a level shown as "spot" or described in words?

When the research couldn't find a current futures price, it either gives the spot (cash) price and labels it, or describes the level, such as "pullback to the 20-day average", rather than guess a number.

Can I share the site with someone else?

Ask first. The circle is kept small on purpose, and every new person needs the code.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's one person's research and opinions, shared for discussion. Make your own decisions, and talk to a licensed professional about your situation.

Futures and options involve substantial risk of loss and aren't suitable for everyone. Past results, including those in the trade journal, don't guarantee future results.
